Replacing the exhaust on a Zip 4T is a short job: two nuts hold the manifold to the cylinder head and two screws hold the muffler to the engine housing. The part that catches people out is the **gasket** at the head flange — this guide tells you exactly when you need one, what to order, and how to fit it so the joint does not leak.

## Applies To

Piaggio / Vespa **Zip 50 4T** and **Zip 100 4T** with carburettor. The 2-stroke Zip uses a different exhaust and is not covered here. Fuel-injected variants are similar mechanically but carry an extra sensor — see the Notes section.

<Callout type="danger">
  **Safety:** The exhaust stays hot long after the engine stops. Let the scooter cool for at least an hour. Wear gloves, and support the scooter on the centre stand on level ground.

## Do I need a gasket?

**Yes — always fit a new one.** The exhaust-to-head joint seals on a single-use gasket (a crush ring or flat gasket in copper/graphite). It flattens to the head face the first time you tighten it and will not seal properly a second time. Reusing it is the most common cause of a "ticking" leak, popping on overrun, and a lean mixture afterwards.

| Situation | Gasket needed |
|-----------|---------------|
| Replacing the whole exhaust (header + muffler) | Head-flange gasket — new |
| Replacing only the muffler, header stays on | Only if the muffler is a slip-fit joint with its own seal; otherwise none |
| Just removing the exhaust to access the engine | New head-flange gasket on refit |

<Callout type="warn">
  **Order by part number, not by "Zip 4T".** The flange gasket differs between the 50 and 100 engines and between model years. Look up your frame number (VIN) in the Piaggio parts catalogue, or match the old gasket against the new one before fitting. Do not guess — the exact OEM number is not listed in the service manual.
</Callout>

## Parts & Materials

| Part | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Complete exhaust (header + muffler) or muffler only | Match to 50 vs 100 and to your year; check for the catalytic/secondary-air variant |
| Head-flange gasket | New every time |
| Manifold nuts (2×) | Replace if rounded or corroded; use new self-locking nuts if the originals are the locking type |
| Muffler mounting screws (2×) | Reuse if the threads are clean |
| Heat-shield / guard screws | Only if the guard is being replaced |
| Copper anti-seize | Thin film on the studs only — never on the gasket face |

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Step 1 — Prepare

1. Let the engine cool completely and put the scooter on its centre stand.
2. Spray penetrating oil on the two manifold nuts and the muffler screws. Leave it for 10 minutes; exhaust hardware is often seized.
3. Remove any body panel or heat-shield that blocks access to the muffler.

### Step 2 — Remove the old exhaust

1. Undo the **two nuts** holding the manifold to the cylinder head. Loosen them evenly, a little at a time.
2. Undo the **two screws** that hold the muffler to the engine housing.
3. Lower the whole muffler assembly out. Watch the interference between the muffler's support bracket and the cooling cover — you may need to angle it out.
4. If your model has a **secondary air (SAS) pipe** on the muffler, disconnect it first.
5. Peel off the old gasket. If it is stuck, do not use a screwdriver on the head; use a plastic scraper.

<Callout type="tip">
  If a stud snaps, stop. Removing a broken stud from an aluminium head needs a stud extractor or a helicoil-type repair. Do not drill freehand.
</Callout>

### Step 3 — Clean and inspect

1. Clean the exhaust port face on the head with a brass brush. It must be flat and free of carbon.
2. Check the studs for damage. Clean the threads.
3. Check the head face for a burnt or eroded groove — that is where an old leak has been. A deep groove needs a new head gasket face treatment or head replacement.
4. Look inside the port for oil or wet carbon. That points to a piston-ring or valve-guide issue, not the exhaust.

### Step 4 — Fit the new exhaust

1. Put a **new gasket** on the studs. It should sit flat against the head with the exhaust port hole aligned.
2. Apply a thin film of copper anti-seize to the studs (not the gasket).
3. Offer the exhaust up and start the two manifold nuts by hand.
4. Fit the two muffler mounting screws **loosely** — do not tighten yet.
5. Tighten the manifold nuts evenly, alternating one and the other, so the flange pulls down square. The service manual gives no manifold-nut torque; as a guide for M6 flange nuts use **~8–10 Nm** and do not exceed it on the aluminium head.
6. Tighten the muffler screws last. This lets the muffler settle without stressing the manifold joint.
7. Refit the guard, heat shield and any bodywork.

### Step 5 — Check for leaks

1. Start the engine and let it idle. Listen for a ticking or puffing sound at the head.
2. Hold a piece of card or a gloved hand near the joint (careful — it heats fast) or use soapy water on a cool-ish joint to look for bubbles.
3. Re-check the manifold nuts once the engine has warmed and cooled once; they can loosen as the gasket beds in.

## After Fitting — Carburettor Notes

A stock replacement exhaust should not need re-jetting. If you fitted an **aftermarket** exhaust, expect a lean mixture: watch for a hesitant throttle, popping on deceleration and a white spark plug tip. Recheck idle speed (Zip 100 carb: about 1,500 ± 150 rpm, CO 3.2 ± 0.5 % at idle per the service manual) and consult the carburettor jetting guides.

## Common Problems

| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---------|--------------|
| Ticking at the head, worse cold | Gasket not seated, nuts too loose, or old gasket reused |
| Popping on overrun | Exhaust leak at the flange or muffler joint |
| Rattle from under the seat | Muffler bracket touching the cooling cover; loose guard |
| Loss of power after replacement | Wrong muffler for the model, or a blocked catalytic silencer |

## Notes on Variants

- **Catalytic / Euro 2–3 silencers** contain a catalyst. Never run the scooter with the catalyst removed if you are subject to emissions inspection.
- **Secondary-air version:** the muffler has a secondary-air connection; reconnect the pipe on refit.
- **EFI Zip:** the exhaust may carry an oxygen sensor. Unplug it before removal and use a sensor-safe anti-seize.
