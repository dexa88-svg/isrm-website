## Introduction

The rear wheel on a GY6 scooter is held on a single axle nut. With the right socket, removing it is straightforward and takes under an hour. This guide covers the full procedure: loosening the axle, removing the wheel, changing or patching the inner tube, fitting a replacement tyre if needed, and reassembling everything correctly. The process applies to all GY6 139QMB (50 cc), 152QMI (125 cc), and 157QMJ (150 cc) variants, including BTC Riva 50, La Souris Sourini/City, and Santini Capri models.

<Callout type="warn">
  Work on a flat, stable surface. Place the scooter on its centre stand before starting. Never work under a scooter supported only by its side stand.
</Callout>

## Tyre Specifications (common GY6 sizes)

| Engine | Front | Rear |
|--------|-------|------|
| GY6 50cc (139QMB) | 3.00-10 or 90/90-10 | 3.50-10 or 100/90-10 |
| GY6 125/150cc (QMI/QMJ) | 90/90-10 | 100/90-10 or 110/80-10 |

Always confirm the tyre size printed on the sidewall of the original tyre before purchasing a replacement.

## Tools & Parts Needed

- 17 mm or 19 mm socket with breaker bar (axle nut — check your specific model; most GY6 50 cc use 17 mm, 125/150 cc often 19 mm)
- 8 mm spanner for brake cable adjuster
- 2–3 plastic or metal tyre levers
- Valve core removal tool
- Bicycle or compressor pump
- Patch kit with rubber cement and patches (if repairing rather than replacing the tube)
- Replacement inner tube (if fitting new — match the size printed on the tyre)
- Replacement tyre (if worn or damaged)
- Torque wrench capable of 40–55 Nm

## Step-by-Step Procedure

### Step 1 — Deflate the Tyre

Remove the valve cap and use the valve core tool to fully deflate the tyre before attempting wheel removal. A deflated tyre makes it easier to break the bead and fit the wheel back through the chain/brake assembly.

### Step 2 — Loosen the Brake Adjuster

On drum-brake GY6 models (most 50 cc), the rear drum brake cable passes through an adjuster nut on the brake plate. Loosen this adjuster fully with an 8 mm spanner so the brake arm has maximum slack — this is essential for sliding the axle out.

On disc-brake models the caliper stays on the swingarm and the wheel simply slides off the rotor.

### Step 3 — Loosen the Axle Nut

With the scooter on its centre stand and the rear wheel still on the ground, place your 17 mm or 19 mm socket on the axle nut (right side of the wheel). Break the nut loose with the breaker bar — it is torqued tightly from the factory. Do not remove it fully yet.

<Callout type="tip">
  If the nut is very stiff, a brief spray of penetrating fluid (WD-40 or equivalent) and a 5-minute wait will help. Do not use heat near the tyre or fuel lines.
</Callout>

### Step 4 — Support the Rear of the Scooter

Once the axle nut is loose, place a hydraulic floor jack or a sturdy block under the rear frame rail to lift the rear wheel clear of the ground. Alternatively, have an assistant hold the scooter while you work — do not rely on the centre stand alone once the axle is removed.

### Step 5 — Remove the Axle Nut and Axle

Remove the axle nut completely and retain any washers or spacers in the order they came off — photograph them if helpful. Slide the axle bolt out from the left side of the swingarm. On some models the axle is threaded at one end only; on others it passes through freely.

### Step 6 — Remove the Rear Wheel

Slide the wheel rearward out of the swingarm. On drum-brake models, disconnect the brake arm rod or cable from the brake plate (pull the rod pin or unclip the cable end) to allow the wheel to come free. Keep track of any chain tensioner or spacer plates that sit between the wheel hub and swingarm.

### Step 7 — Remove the Tyre from the Rim

Lay the wheel flat. Insert a tyre lever between the tyre bead and the rim at the valve hole and push the bead over the rim edge. Work around the tyre with a second lever, keeping the first in place. Once one side of the bead is fully off, push the valve core inward and pull the inner tube out from between the tyre and rim.

<Callout type="warn">
  Metal tyre levers can damage the rim if used aggressively. Use smooth, controlled movements and keep levers away from the valve stem area.
</Callout>

### Step 8 — Locate the Puncture (if repairing the tube)

Inflate the tube slightly. Submerge it in a tub of water and look for a stream of bubbles, or simply listen and feel for escaping air. Mark the puncture location with a pen. Once found, deflate the tube fully before patching.

To patch: rough up the area around the hole with sandpaper (included in most kits), apply a thin, even coat of rubber cement and let it dry for 2–3 minutes until tacky, then press the patch firmly down and hold it for at least 60 seconds. Inflate the tube to check the repair holds before refitting.

### Step 9 — Fit the New or Repaired Tube

Inflate the replacement or repaired tube just enough to give it shape (not firm). Dust it lightly with talcum powder if available — this prevents pinching. Feed the valve stem through the valve hole in the rim first, then work the tube evenly inside the tyre cavity all the way around.

### Step 10 — Refit the Tyre Bead

Starting at the valve, push the tyre bead back over the rim edge by hand, working away from the valve in both directions. The last section near the valve is hardest — use a tyre lever carefully if needed, checking that the inner tube is not caught between the lever and rim. Once both beads are seated, check the valve stem is straight and not pulled to one side.

<Callout type="warn">
  Before inflating, run your fingers around both sides of the rim and confirm the tube is not pinched between the tyre bead and the rim edge. A pinched tube will puncture immediately on inflation.
</Callout>

### Step 11 — Inflate to Correct Pressure

Inflate the tyre to the recommended pressure. Most GY6 50 cc models specify **1.75–2.0 bar (25–29 psi)** at the rear; 125/150 cc models typically **2.0–2.25 bar (29–33 psi)** depending on load. Check your owner's manual or the label inside the frame for the specific value.

| Model size | Recommended rear tyre pressure |
|------------|-------------------------------|
| GY6 50cc | 1.75–2.0 bar (25–29 psi) |
| GY6 125cc | 2.0–2.25 bar (29–33 psi) |
| GY6 150cc | 2.0–2.25 bar (29–33 psi) |

### Step 12 — Refit the Wheel

Slide the wheel back into the swingarm, aligning the brake arm rod with the brake plate pin (drum brake) or the disc into the caliper (disc brake). Replace any spacers or chain tensioner plates in their original positions. Feed the axle back through from the left side.

### Step 13 — Reinstall and Torque the Axle Nut

Thread the axle nut on by hand first, then torque it to **40–55 Nm** (check your service manual for your specific model). Most GY6 50 cc axle nuts are torqued to 45 Nm; 150 cc variants to 50–55 Nm.

<Callout type="warn">
  An undertorqued axle nut can work loose while riding, causing catastrophic loss of the rear wheel. Always use a torque wrench.
</Callout>

### Step 14 — Readjust the Rear Brake

On drum-brake models, re-tighten the brake cable adjuster until there is approximately 10–15 mm of free play at the brake lever or pedal. Check that the wheel spins freely and the brake does not drag.

On disc-brake models, pump the brake lever a few times before riding to re-seat the caliper piston.

### Step 15 — Test Ride

Lower the scooter off the jack or stand. Check tyre pressure once more (pressure can change slightly after the tyre seats fully). Ride slowly and brake gently for the first few stops to confirm correct brake function before resuming normal riding.
