## Introduction

The Vespa Primavera and Sprint (2013–present, all displacements) use a single-piston sliding caliper on the front wheel. Brake pads typically last 8,000–15,000 km depending on riding style and terrain. Squealing brakes and a spongy lever are the most common signs that pads need replacing. This guide covers front disc pads; the rear drum brake shoes on these models require a separate procedure.

<Callout type="warn">
  Never ride with worn pads past the minimum thickness (1 mm). Metal-on-rotor contact destroys the disc and leads to brake failure.
</Callout>

## Tools & Parts Needed

- 8 mm and 10 mm socket wrench set
- Flat-head screwdriver (for pad retaining pin extraction)
- Brake piston retraction tool or C-clamp with flat plate
- Brake cleaner spray
- Clean lint-free rags
- Replacement brake pads (OEM Piaggio or equivalent sintered pads for street use)
- Latex gloves

**Recommended OEM replacement pads**: Piaggio part number 647059 (front, single-disc models, 2013–present Primavera/Sprint). Confirm fitment with your VIN before ordering.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

### Step 1 — Safety Preparation

1. Place the scooter on its centre stand on level ground.
2. Allow the brake system to cool if the scooter was recently ridden.
3. Put on latex gloves — brake dust and old pad material are harmful if inhaled or absorbed through skin.

### Step 2 — Inspect Current Pad Thickness

1. Look through the inspection window on the caliper body (the gap between caliper and disc).
2. Each pad should have at least 1 mm of friction material remaining above the backing plate.
3. If either pad is at or below 1 mm, proceed with replacement.

<Callout type="tip">
  Even if one pad appears fine, always replace both pads as a matched pair to ensure even braking force.
</Callout>

### Step 3 — Remove the Caliper

1. Locate the two caliper slide-pin bolts on the rear face of the caliper (8 mm or 10 mm, depending on year).
2. Support the caliper with one hand and unscrew both bolts.
3. Slide the caliper off the disc — do not let it hang from the brake line. Secure it to the fork leg with a cable tie or rest it on a rag on the tyre.

<Callout type="warn">
  Never press the brake lever with the caliper removed. This will push the piston fully out and require bleeding the system to reset.
</Callout>

### Step 4 — Extract the Old Pads

1. The pads are held by a retaining pin or clip through the caliper body. Use a flat-head screwdriver to push out the retaining pin.
2. Slide both pads out of the caliper bracket.
3. Inspect the rotor surface — light grooves are normal; deep scoring (grooves you can feel with a fingernail) means the rotor needs replacing alongside the pads.

### Step 5 — Retract the Caliper Piston

New pads are thicker than worn pads. The caliper piston must be pushed back to make room.

1. Place the old pad (or a flat piece of metal) against the piston face to protect it.
2. Use a C-clamp or piston retraction tool to slowly compress the piston flush into the caliper body.
3. As the piston retracts, brake fluid is pushed back into the reservoir. Check the reservoir level — remove excess fluid with a syringe if it overflows.

<Callout type="tip">
  If the piston won't retract, the caliper slide pins may be corroded and binding. Clean and lubricate them with silicone grease before reinstallation.
</Callout>

### Step 6 — Clean the Caliper

1. Spray brake cleaner on the caliper bracket, pad channels, and slide pins.
2. Wipe clean with lint-free rags.
3. Apply a thin film of silicone-based brake caliper grease to the slide pin bores and the backs of the pad backing plates (not the friction material).
4. Never apply grease to the rotor or pad faces.

### Step 7 — Install New Pads

1. Insert the new pads into the caliper bracket. The inner pad (piston side) and outer pad are typically identical on single-piston designs.
2. Reinsert the retaining pin and ensure it is fully seated.
3. Slide the caliper back over the rotor, ensuring the pads are correctly seated on both sides of the disc.

### Step 8 — Reinstall Caliper Bolts

1. Thread both caliper slide-pin bolts in by hand first to avoid cross-threading.
2. Torque to specification:

| Fastener | Torque |
|----------|--------|
| Caliper slide-pin bolts | 25–28 Nm |

### Step 9 — Bed In the New Pads

New brake pads require a bedding-in process to transfer a thin, even layer of friction material onto the rotor.

1. Start with 5–6 moderate stops from 30 km/h, allowing the brakes to cool between each.
2. Progress to 5–6 firmer stops from 50 km/h.
3. Avoid aggressive stops for the first 100 km.

Squealing during the first 50 km is normal as the pad surface conditions. If squealing persists beyond 100 km, check pad-to-rotor contact and ensure the slide pins are moving freely.

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Minimum pad thickness | 1 mm |
| Minimum rotor thickness (OEM disc) | 3.5 mm |
| Caliper slide-pin bolt torque | 25–28 Nm |
| Brake fluid type | DOT 4 (Primavera/Sprint) |
| Rotor diameter (Primavera 125/150) | 220 mm |

## Eliminating Brake Squeal

If brakes squeal after new pads are fitted and bedded in:

- Verify the caliper slide pins are clean and lubricated — seized pins prevent the pad from releasing cleanly.
- Apply anti-squeal compound (copper grease or specific brake shim compound) to the **back** of the pad backing plate only.
- Check that the rotor is clean and free of glazing — lightly scuff with 120-grit sandpaper if glazed.
- Ensure the caliper mounting bracket is torqued correctly and not loose.
