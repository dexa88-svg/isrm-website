## How the GY6 CVT Works

The Continuously Variable Transmission (CVT) on GY6 scooters consists of three main assemblies: the **variator** (primary pulley on the crankshaft), the **rear clutch bell/secondary pulley** (on the transmission input shaft), and the **drive belt** that runs between them. As engine RPM rises, centrifugal rollers inside the variator push the pulley faces together, forcing the belt to ride higher — effectively changing the gear ratio automatically without any driver input.

## When to Replace

<table><tbody><tr><th>Component</th><th>Replacement Interval</th><th>Symptoms of Wear</th></tr><tr><td>Drive belt</td><td>Every 8,000–12,000 km (or sooner if cracked)</td><td>Slipping, loss of acceleration, jerky takeoff, visible cracks on belt edges</td></tr><tr><td>Variator rollers</td><td>Every 10,000–15,000 km</td><td>Hesitation at mid-throttle, flat acceleration curve, rattling from CVT cover</td></tr><tr><td>Clutch springs</td><td>Every 15,000–20,000 km</td><td>Clutch engaging too early (creep at idle), poor low-speed drivability</td></tr><tr><td>CVT cover gasket</td><td>Whenever cover is removed</td><td>Oil/grease seeping from CVT cover seam</td></tr></tbody></table>

<Callout type="warn">
  **Safety first:** Work on a cold engine only. The variator and clutch bell reach high temperatures during operation. Ensure the scooter is on its centre stand on a flat, stable surface.
</Callout>

## Part 1 — Belt Removal Without Special Tools

This section covers removing and replacing the drive belt only, using a technique that avoids the need for a variator holding tool or impact wrench. Useful when you only need a belt swap and don't intend to service the variator or rollers.

### Step-by-Step: Belt Only

**Step 1 — Remove the CVT Cover**

1. Place the scooter on its centre stand on level ground.
2. Locate the CVT cover on the left side of the engine (the large plastic or alloy cover with ventilation slots).
3. Remove all cover bolts — typically 6–8 bolts of 8 mm or 10 mm. Keep them together; they are often different lengths.
4. Gently tap the cover with a soft mallet to break the gasket seal if it is stuck. Do not pry against the casting — it cracks easily.
5. Remove the cover. Inspect inside for belt dust, cracked rubber fragments, or oil contamination (indicates a crankshaft seal leak).

**Step 2 — Remove the Drive Belt**

1. With the cover off, you will see the drive belt running between the two pulleys.
2. Compress (push together) the faces of the rear clutch pulley by hand while simultaneously lifting the belt off the front variator pulley. This creates slack in the belt loop.
3. Slide the belt off the rear clutch, then off the front variator. If the belt is stiff or cracked, it may snap during removal — wear eye protection.
4. Inspect the belt: cracks along the edge, fraying, glazing, or visible rubber chunks missing all indicate the belt needs replacement.

**Step 3 — Install the New Belt & Reassemble**

1. Route the new belt over the rear clutch pulley first, ensuring it sits centrally in the groove.
2. While compressing the rear pulley faces together, loop the belt over the front variator. Release the rear pulley — the spring tension will pull the belt taut.
3. Rotate the rear wheel by hand to seat the belt evenly and check that it tracks centrally on both pulleys without twisting.
4. Clean the CVT cover interior and replace the gasket if torn or compressed flat.
5. Refit the CVT cover. Tighten bolts in a cross pattern to approximately **10–12 Nm**. Do not overtighten — the cover threads strip easily.

<Callout type="tip">
  **Break-in tip:** After fitting a new belt, avoid full-throttle acceleration for the first 100–200 km. New belts need time to seat and conform to the pulley surfaces. Premature hard acceleration can glaze or crack a new belt prematurely.
</Callout>

## Part 2 — Variator Pulley Disassembly & Roller Replacement

This section covers full variator removal, inspection of the pulley ramps, and roller replacement. Required when rollers are worn, you're tuning for performance, or belt-only replacement hasn't resolved your CVT symptoms.

**Additional tools required for this section:**
- Variator holding tool (universal GY6 type) — do not attempt to jam the variator with a screwdriver
- Impact wrench or breaker bar (the variator nut is torqued very tight)
- Torque wrench

### Step-by-Step: Variator & Rollers

**Step 1 — Remove the CVT Cover & Belt**

Follow Part 1, Steps 1 and 2 above to remove the cover and belt first.

**Step 2 — Remove the Variator**

1. Use the variator holding tool to lock the front pulley against rotation.
2. With an impact wrench or breaker bar, remove the variator nut. Note: threads vary by manufacturer — if the nut won't loosen counter-clockwise, try clockwise (some GY6 variants use a standard right-hand thread). This nut is typically torqued to **49 Nm (36 ft-lb)**.
3. Slide off the outer face of the variator. Note the orientation — there is often an arrow or chamfer indicating the correct fitting direction.
4. Lift off the outer pulley face and set it aside.

**Step 3 — Inspect & Replace Rollers**

1. Remove the rollers from the guide cage. There are typically 6 rollers. Inspect each one: flat spots, grooves worn into the surface, or irregular wear all indicate replacement is needed.
2. **Roller specifications by engine:**
   - GY6 50cc (139QMB): **16×13 mm** @ 7.5–8.5 g (OEM stock; aftermarket range 5–11.5 g)
   - GY6 125cc (152QMI): **18×14 mm** @ 12–13 g (OEM ~12.8 g)
   - GY6 150cc (157QMJ): **18×14 mm** @ 12–14 g

   Always use the correct diameter for your engine — mixing sizes causes CVT failure. Heavier rollers delay engagement (better top speed); lighter rollers engage earlier (better acceleration). Do not deviate more than 2 g from stock unless tuning intentionally.
3. Inspect the variator ramp surfaces for grooving or cracks. Shallow grooves from flat-spotted rollers are normal; deep grooves mean the variator body needs replacement.

**Step 4 — Inspect the Rear Clutch (while you're in there)**

1. Inspect the clutch bell interior for glazing (shiny hard surface) or scoring. Light glazing can be scuffed with coarse sandpaper; heavy scoring means replacement.
2. Check the clutch shoes for uneven wear. The shoes should contact the bell evenly around the circumference.

**Step 5 — Reassemble**

1. Clean the variator ramp surfaces and roller guides with a dry rag. **Do not apply grease to the rollers** — they must run dry. Grease attracts belt dust and accelerates wear.
2. Install new rollers in the guide cage.
3. Reinstall the outer variator face in the correct orientation and torque the nut to **49 Nm**.
4. Refit the belt (Part 1, Step 3) and CVT cover.
