The combined TPS (Throttle Position Sensor) and MAP (Manifold Absolute Pressure) sensor is the most frequently replaced sensor on GY6 EFI scooters, and the most-discussed failure point in Euro 4/5 forums. The TPS is a potentiometer that wears fastest at the low end of its travel — exactly the idle position — producing erratic or out-of-range voltage signals that cause the ECU to cut fuel, misfire, or enter limp-home mode. This guide covers disassembly, replacement, and the critical step of setting the correct idle output voltage on the new sensor.

## Applies To

GY6 139QMB (50cc) and 157QMJ (150cc) with Rongmao MT5 ECU (Euro 4/5 EFI), including the BTC Riva EFI, La Souris Sourini/City EFI, Santini Capri EFI, and AGM VX50 EFI.

## Background: What the TPS/MAP Sensor Does

The TPS sends a voltage signal to the ECU that corresponds to throttle angle — 0.63–0.73 V at closed throttle (idle), rising to approximately 4.5 V at full open. The ECU uses this to determine fuelling mode: idle, part-throttle, or wide-open. At closed throttle, if the voltage is outside the expected idle window, the ECU cannot correctly identify the idle state and may cut fuel — causing a stall.

The MAP sensor measures intake manifold vacuum and works in conjunction with the TPS to calculate the engine's air-mass intake. On the combined unit, both sensors share a housing mounted to the throttle body.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Step 1 — Prepare and access the throttle body

1. Park on centre stand, engine off, fully cooled.
2. Disconnect the **battery negative (–) terminal**.
3. Remove body panels as needed to access the throttle body — on most GY6 EFI scooters, the throttle body is visible once the under-seat storage or floorboard trim is removed (3–5 Phillips screws).

### Step 2 — Locate and photograph the sensor

The TPS/MAP sensor is bolted to the side of the throttle body — a roughly rectangular or oval plastic unit, usually on the right side of the throttle body when viewed from behind.

1. Before removing anything, take a clear photograph of:
   - The sensor position and orientation
   - The connector and its routing
   - Any wiring clips holding the harness

This is your reference for reassembly.

### Step 3 — Disconnect the wiring connector

1. Press the connector locking tab (small plastic tab on the side or top of the connector).
2. Pull the connector straight out — do not twist or lever it.
3. Inspect the connector pins: green corrosion or bent pins can cause the same symptoms as a failed sensor. Clean with electrical contact cleaner if corrosion is present.

### Step 4 — Remove the sensor

The sensor is typically retained by 2 small screws (M4, Phillips or flat-head) threaded into the throttle body body.

1. Undo both screws fully and set aside — they are small and easy to lose.
2. Gently twist and pull the sensor away from the throttle body. There may be a small O-ring or gasket between sensor and throttle body — remove it and discard (fit new one if supplied with replacement).
3. Inspect the throttle body bore where the sensor mounts for any debris or damage.

<Callout type="warn">
  ⚠️ Do not force the sensor — if it doesn't pull free easily, check for a hidden third screw or a clip. Forcing it can crack the throttle body.
</Callout>

### Step 5 — Fit the new sensor (loose — do not tighten yet)

1. Fit the new O-ring or gasket to the sensor face.
2. Locate the sensor onto the throttle body shaft and bore — the sensor has a D-shaped hole that engages with the throttle shaft flat; it should drop into position without force.
3. Insert the retaining screws finger-tight only. **Do not tighten yet** — the sensor position must be adjusted before locking down.

### Step 6 — Set idle voltage (critical step)

This is the most important step. The TPS output at closed throttle target depends on your ECU:

- **Rongmao MT5** (most Euro 4/5 GY6 50cc): **0.63–0.73 V** at closed throttle
- **Delphi MT05**: **0.50–0.55 V** at closed throttle

Check the ECU label under the seat panel to confirm which system your scooter has. The procedure is the same regardless of target voltage.

1. Reconnect the battery negative terminal.
2. Reconnect the TPS/MAP connector.
3. Turn ignition to **ON** (do not start the engine).
4. Set your multimeter to **DC Volts, 2 V range**.
5. Identify the TPS signal wire at the connector (refer to your model's wiring diagram; typically the middle pin of a 3-pin connector — the outer two are 5 V supply and ground).
6. Back-probe the signal wire with your multimeter (a thin wire or pin pushed alongside the connector pin without disconnecting it).
7. Read the voltage with the throttle fully **closed** (at rest).

**Adjust the sensor position:**

- If the reading is **above 0.73 V** — rotate the sensor body slightly anti-clockwise (as viewed from the front) to lower the output.
- If the reading is **below 0.63 V** — rotate the sensor body slightly clockwise.
- Work in tiny increments (1–2 mm of rotation makes a significant voltage difference).

8. Once the reading is within **0.63–0.73 V**, check that opening the throttle smoothly raises the voltage with no flat spots or drops. The voltage should sweep cleanly from ~0.68 V (closed) to ~4.4 V (full open).
9. Tighten the retaining screws firmly but not over-torqued — 1.5–2 N·m.
10. Recheck voltage after tightening — tightening can shift the sensor slightly.

<Callout type="tip">
  💡 **Tip:** The target is the centre of the range: **0.68 V** is ideal. A value right at the boundary (0.63 V or 0.73 V) may drift out of spec as the sensor warms.
</Callout>

### Step 7 — Test and reassemble

1. Start the engine and allow it to warm fully.
2. Confirm idle is stable at 1,400–1,600 RPM.
3. Blip the throttle several times — the RPM should return cleanly to idle without stalling.
4. Refit all body panels in reverse order.
5. Reconnect the battery negative.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| Voltage won't reach 0.63–0.73 V anywhere in sensor rotation | Replacement sensor is wrong part or faulty | Verify part number; test on bench with 5 V supply |
| Engine stalls on deceleration despite correct idle voltage | Voltage drops below 0.5 V during throttle close — flat spot | Sweep throttle slowly and watch for voltage dip; replace again if fault repeats |
| Check engine light remains after replacement | ECU fault code stored — not auto-cleared on all models | Disconnect battery for 5 minutes to clear stored codes |
| New sensor physically doesn't fit | Wrong connector or shaft bore | Check shaft diameter (usually 5 mm) and connector pin count against the original |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I clean the old TPS instead of replacing it?**  
A: The TPS wiper track wears physically — cleaning the contacts may temporarily improve the signal but the underlying wear remains. On a sensor that reads erratically, replacement is the only reliable fix.

**Q: The replacement came without an O-ring — is that okay?**  
A: It depends on the model. Some sensors seal against the throttle body with an O-ring, others with a flat gasket, others with no seal (the bore is not vacuum-referenced at that point). Reuse the old O-ring if it is in good condition.

**Q: Does the ECU need reprogramming after a TPS replacement?**  
A: No. Setting the physical idle voltage as described above is sufficient. The Rongmao MT5 ECU uses the voltage directly — there is no TPS "learn procedure" required.
