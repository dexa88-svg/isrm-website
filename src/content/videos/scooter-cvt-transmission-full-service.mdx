A properly maintained CVT (Continuously Variable Transmission) is essential for smooth power delivery and consistent performance on all GY6 and Piaggio 4-stroke scooters. The CVT converts engine speed to wheel speed through a pair of pulleys connected by a drive belt — the front (drive) variator opens and closes based on centrifugal force from roller weights, while the rear clutch/driven pulley responds to belt tension. This video guides you through a complete CVT service inspection.

## Key Service Points

### Roller Weights

Roller weights are the small cylindrical or barrel-shaped weights (typically 6 per variator on GY6 engines, 6 per variator on Piaggio) that determine how and when the variator opens. As the engine speeds up, centrifugal force pushes the rollers outward against the variator ramp, pushing the front pulley halves apart and forcing the belt to ride higher (increasing the effective ratio for more speed).

**Common signs of worn rollers:**
- Jerky or stuttering acceleration, particularly from a standstill
- Engine revs high but speed does not increase proportionally
- Vibration through the floorboard at a specific speed
- Flat spots on rollers visible on inspection

**Roller weights for common GY6 engines:**

<table><tbody>
<tr><th>Engine</th><th>Standard Roller Weight</th><th>Standard Diameter</th></tr>
<tr><td>GY6 139QMB (50cc)</td><td>6 g (some variants 7 g)</td><td>15×12 mm</td></tr>
<tr><td>GY6 157QMJ (125/150cc)</td><td>11 g (some variants 10 g or 12 g)</td><td>18×14 mm or 20×12 mm</td></tr>
<tr><td>Piaggio Zip 50 4T / Zip 100</td><td>5.5 g–7 g depending on variant</td><td>15×12 mm</td></tr>
</tbody></table>

Always replace all rollers as a set — mixing old and new rollers causes uneven loading and accelerated wear of the variator ramp faces.

### Drive Belt

The GY6 drive belt is a reinforced rubber V-belt that runs between the front variator and the rear driven pulley. It wears by getting narrower — as the belt wears, it rides lower in the pulleys, effectively changing the transmission ratio and reducing top speed.

<Callout type="warn">
**Belt width is the critical measurement.** A worn belt that still looks intact externally may already be below the minimum width specification. Always measure belt width with a calliper, not just by eye. Standard GY6 157QMJ belt width is typically 17.0 mm new; replace when below 15.5 mm. For 139QMB (50cc): 17.0 mm new, replace below 15.5 mm. Check your service manual for the exact figures.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
**Cheap belts fail fast.** Use OEM-specification or well-known aftermarket brands (NCY, Malossi, Gates, Polini). A low-quality belt can snap without warning within a few hundred kilometres on a stock engine — leaving you stranded with a locked-up transmission.
</Callout>

### Clutch Assembly (Rear Pulley)

The rear driven pulley assembly combines the driven pulley halves, the torque spring, and the centrifugal clutch bell. At idle, the clutch pads are retracted inside the bell — the engine can rev without moving the scooter. As engine rpm rises, centrifugal force expands the pads against the clutch bell, engaging drive to the rear wheel.

Replace clutch pads when:
- Lining material is thinner than **2 mm**
- Lining is glazed (shiny, hard surface) — caused by slipping at low rpm; light sanding may temporarily restore grip, but replacement is recommended
- Pads show uneven wear (one side more worn than the other) — indicates a bent or worn clutch spider

Replace clutch springs at the same time as pads to ensure consistent engagement behaviour.
