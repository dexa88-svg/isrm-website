The Throttle Position Sensor (TPS) is a variable resistor mounted on the throttle body of GY6 EFI scooters. As you open the throttle, a sliding contact inside the TPS changes resistance, sending a voltage signal to the ECU that represents throttle opening angle. The ECU uses this data — alongside the MAP sensor and O2 sensor — to calculate the correct fuel injection duration and ignition timing.

On Euro 4 and Euro 5 GY6 50cc scooters (2017 onwards), the TPS is part of the throttle body assembly used by the Delphi MT05 or Motion EFI systems. A faulty TPS causes erratic fuelling, stuttering, poor idle, or a complete no-start with a CEL (Check Engine Light) flashing.

## Fault Codes for TPS Faults

Most GY6 EFI systems flash a code sequence via the CEL/MIL light. On Delphi MT05-based systems:

<tr><th>DTC Code</th><th>Meaning</th><th>CEL Blink Pattern</th></tr>
<tr><td>P0121</td><td>TPS signal out of range — intermittent</td><td>1 long, 2 short</td></tr>
<tr><td>P0122</td><td>TPS circuit low (below 0.2 V)</td><td>1 long, 2 short (variant)</td></tr>

## Symptoms of a Failing TPS

- Engine stutters or hesitates when opening throttle from idle
- Erratic idle speed — surging up and down
- Poor fuel economy or black exhaust smoke (ECU over-fuelling due to incorrect TPS signal)
- CEL/MIL illuminated — flashing P0121, P0122, or P0123
- Engine runs fine at idle but cuts out under load
- Hard starting (ECU applies incorrect starting enrichment)

## Step 1 — Visual Inspection

Before testing with a multimeter, inspect the TPS physically:

1. Locate the throttle body on the intake side of the engine. The TPS is a small sensor plugged into the side of the throttle body with a 3-wire connector.
2. Unplug the connector and inspect both the sensor pins and the harness connector for:
   - Corrosion (green or white residue on the pins)
   - Bent or pushed-back pins
   - Cracked or chafed wiring near the connector
3. Reconnect and wiggle the connector while the engine idles. If this changes idle behaviour, the connection is intermittent — clean and re-pin before replacing the sensor.

## Step 2 — Voltage Test (Live Signal)

This test checks whether the TPS is producing a correct, smooth voltage output as the throttle is opened.

**Setup:**
1. With the ignition ON and engine off (or running), back-probe the TPS connector with a multimeter set to DC Voltage.
2. Identify the three wires:
   - **5 V reference (Vref)** — from ECU, typically green or white wire
   - **Ground** — black wire
   - **Signal output** — yellow, orange, or blue wire (goes back to ECU)

3. Measure between the Signal wire and Ground wire.

**Expected readings:**

<table><tbody>
<tr><th>Throttle Position</th><th>Expected Voltage</th></tr>
<tr><td>Fully closed (idle)</td><td>0.4–0.7 V</td></tr>
<tr><td>Half throttle (~45°)</td><td>~2.0–2.5 V</td></tr>
<tr><td>Wide open throttle (WOT)</td><td>4.0–4.8 V</td></tr>
</tbody></table>

<Callout type="warn">
**Key test:** Slowly open and close the throttle while watching the multimeter. The voltage must change **smoothly and continuously** — no sudden jumps, flat spots, or drop-outs. Any discontinuity in the sweep means the sliding contact inside the TPS is worn or damaged and the sensor must be replaced.
</Callout>

If the voltage is stuck at 0 V or 5 V regardless of throttle position, check the 5 V reference supply and ground first. If those are correct, the TPS itself is faulty.

## Step 3 — Reference Voltage Check

1. Unplug the TPS connector.
2. With ignition ON, measure between the Vref pin in the harness connector and the Ground pin.
3. Expected: **4.8–5.2 V DC**.
4. If you read 0 V or battery voltage: the ECU or wiring harness has a fault — not the TPS.

## Step 4 — Replacing the TPS

On most GY6 EFI throttle bodies, the TPS is a bolt-on sensor on the throttle body:

1. Unplug the 3-wire TPS connector.
2. Remove the 2 Phillips screws securing the TPS to the throttle body.
3. Slide the old TPS off the throttle shaft.
4. Fit the new TPS — align the slot on the sensor's drive with the throttle shaft flat.
5. Reinstall and tighten the screws finger-tight. **Do not fully tighten yet** — the TPS may need adjustment.

**TPS Adjustment (Closed Throttle Voltage):**

1. With the ignition ON, measure the TPS signal voltage with the throttle fully closed.
2. Rotate the TPS body slightly while watching the voltage until it reads **0.50–0.55 V** (fully closed position per Delphi MT05 spec).
3. Once set, tighten the mounting screws firmly.
4. Recheck: open the throttle to WOT and confirm voltage reaches approximately 4.5 V, then returns to 0.5 V when released.

<Callout type="tip">
**Clear fault codes after replacement.** If you have an OBD2 KKL cable and HUD ECU Hacker, clear any stored P0121–P0123 codes after fitting the new sensor. Without a scanner, disconnect the battery for 5 minutes to reset the ECU — the CEL will extinguish if the fault is resolved.
</Callout>

## Replacement TPS Part Notes

Euro 4/5 GY6 50cc scooters (BTC Riva, La Souris, AGM etc.) use a TPS integrated into the throttle body for the Delphi MT05 or Motion EFI system. The TPS and MAP sensor are often combined into a single unit on these platforms. When ordering a replacement, specify:

- **ECU type:** Delphi MT05 or Motion (check the ECU label under the seat panel)
- **Engine code:** 139QMB (50cc) or 157QMJ (150cc)
- **Euro standard:** Euro 4 or Euro 5
