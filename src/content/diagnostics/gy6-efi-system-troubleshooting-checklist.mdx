## Introduction

If your GY6 EFI scooter won't start, idles roughly, or stalls, work through this checklist in order before replacing parts. Most EFI faults on GY6 scooters come down to a handful of root causes: poor grounding, insufficient fuel pressure, air leaks, or a TPS that needs resetting. This guide applies to BTC Riva, La Souris Sourini, Santini Capri, and other GY6-platform scooters fitted with Rongmao, Delphi, or Rolling Wrench ProBld EFI systems.

<Callout type="warn">
  Always confirm ignition is off and the fuel system is depressurised before disconnecting fuel lines. Fuel sprayed onto a hot engine is a fire hazard.
</Callout>

## Tools & Parts Needed

- Multimeter (DC voltage and resistance)
- Fuel pressure gauge rated to at least 70 PSI (5 bar)
- OBD2 / KKL 6-pin diagnostic cable (for fault code reading)
- Carburetor cleaner spray (air leak detection — do NOT use near flame)
- 8-gauge ground wire and ring terminals

## Step-by-Step Diagnostic Checklist

### Step 1 — Verify Ground Connection

A weak ground is the single most common cause of EFI faults on GY6 scooters.

1. Locate the EFI system's ground wire (usually black, from the ECU harness).
2. Run a dedicated 8-gauge wire from the **negative battery terminal directly to the starter motor mounting bolt**.
3. Connect the EFI system's ground wire to that same bolt, sharing the earthing point.
4. Check with a multimeter: resistance between battery negative and ECU ground should be < 0.2 Ω.

<Callout type="tip">
  Do not rely on chassis grounds through frame bolts painted or corroded with oxidation — always use a direct battery-to-starter dedicated ground.
</Callout>

### Step 2 — Check Fuel Supply to Tank

An air bubble at the inlet can prevent the pump from priming.

1. Confirm the fuel tank is at least one-quarter full.
2. Trace the fuel inlet line from the pump to the tank. **No part of the inlet line should run above the bottom of the fuel tank** — any high loop creates an air bubble the pump cannot overcome.
3. Disconnect the inlet line at the pump and allow fuel to flow briefly onto a rag to purge any air pocket, then reconnect.

### Step 3 — Measure Fuel Pressure at Injector

1. Connect a fuel pressure gauge between the fuel pump outlet and the injector supply line using a T-fitting.
2. Key on (engine not started) — the pump should prime and hold pressure within 5 seconds.
3. **Target pressure: 42 PSI (2.9 bar).** Low pressure indicates a failing pump or clogged filter; high pressure indicates a faulty regulator.

| Pressure | Likely Cause |
|----------|--------------|
| < 35 PSI | Weak pump, clogged inline filter, or air in inlet |
| 42 PSI ± 3 | Normal |
| > 50 PSI | Faulty fuel pressure regulator |

### Step 4 — Check for Air Leaks at Intake

Air leaks cause the ECU to run lean, leading to rough idle, hesitation, or stalling.

<Callout type="warn">
  Use carburetor cleaner for leak detection only with the engine cold or idling at low RPM. Keep a fire extinguisher nearby and do not use near ignition sources.
</Callout>

1. Confirm the plastic intake manifold spacer is **removed** — EFI systems tuned for a specific throttle body bore do not use this spacer.
2. Start the engine and allow it to idle.
3. Spray short bursts of carburetor cleaner around the intake manifold joint, throttle body clocking flange, and any vacuum ports.
4. If engine RPM changes (rises then drops) when sprayed, that location is sucking air — reseal with fresh clamps or RTV sealant and re-test.

### Step 5 — Read Fault Codes (OBD2)

1. With the ignition off, plug the KKL OBD2 cable into the 6-pin diagnostic connector (typically under the seat or near the battery).
2. Open HUD ECU Hacker or a compatible OBD2 app and key on.
3. Navigate to **Fault Codes / DTC** — note all stored codes.
4. Clear codes and re-test after completing other steps. Codes that reappear after clearing indicate active faults.

Common codes and their meanings are documented in the [GY6 EFI OBD-2 DTC Fault Codes guide](/diagnostics/gy6-efi-obd2-dtc-fault-codes.html).

### Step 6 — Reset the Throttle Position Sensor (TPS)

A TPS out of calibration causes idle instability, stalling on deceleration, and hesitation off idle.

1. Key on, engine off.
2. Follow the TPS reset procedure for your ECU variant — typically: key on → slowly open throttle from fully closed to fully open 3 times → key off → wait 10 seconds → key on.
3. Alternatively, connect the OBD2 cable and use the ECU software's **TPS Reset / Throttle Adaptation** function.
4. After reset, start the engine and verify idle stabilises within 30 seconds of warm-up.

For a detailed TPS diagnosis and replacement guide, see [GY6 EFI TPS Sensor Diagnosis & Replacement](/diagnostics/gy6-efi-tps-sensor-diagnosis.html).

### Step 7 — Adjust the Idle Air/Fuel Screw

The finger screw (mixture screw) on the throttle body is a manual trim for the idle zone (below approximately 2,800 RPM). The ECU does not auto-tune at idle.

1. With the engine warm and at idle, turn the screw in (clockwise) by ¼-turn increments and wait 10 seconds after each.
2. Turn back out if idle drops or engine hunts.
3. Find the position where idle RPM is **highest and most stable** — this is the correct setting.
4. If the engine stalls when coming to a stop from road speed, this screw is the adjustment to make.

### Step 8 — Verify Woodruff Key and Magneto Timing

If none of the above resolves a no-start:

1. Remove the left engine cover and inspect the magneto rotor.
2. Confirm the **woodruff key slot on the magneto rotor is fully seated over the woodruff key on the crankshaft**. A displaced woodruff key throws ignition timing off completely and causes no-start with no spark fault code.
3. Remove the rotor with a flywheel puller if needed, inspect the key and slot for damage, and reinstall with the key seated.

<Callout type="danger">
  Never attempt to hammer the rotor onto the crankshaft — this damages the key slot and crankshaft taper. Always use a flywheel puller for removal and hand-tighten plus torque spec for installation (typically 35–40 Nm).
</Callout>

### Step 9 — Confirm Correct EFI Kit for Engine Size

If the scooter was recently converted to EFI or the ECU was replaced:

- A **150cc kit (157QMJ)** uses different injector sizing, fuel maps, and throttle body bore than a **125cc kit (152QMI)**.
- Using the wrong kit results in chronic rich or lean running that no amount of adjustment will correct — the ECU must match the engine displacement.

### Step 10 — Check the Fuel Pump Regulator

A failing pressure regulator allows pressure to bleed off after the pump stops, causing hard hot-start and lean surging.

1. Run the pump until pressure is at 42 PSI.
2. Key off — note pressure on the gauge.
3. If pressure drops below 30 PSI within 2 minutes, the check valve in the pump or the pressure regulator is leaking.
4. Replace the integrated pump/regulator assembly.

## Specifications Summary

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Fuel pressure (static) | 42 PSI (2.9 bar) |
| Ground resistance (battery neg to ECU) | < 0.2 Ω |
| Idle RPM (warm, GY6 150cc EFI) | 1,400–1,800 RPM |
| TPS voltage at idle (closed throttle) | ~0.5 V |
| TPS voltage at WOT (wide open throttle) | ~4.5 V |

## Next Steps if Fault Persists

If all 10 steps pass but the fault remains:

1. Verify the fuel injector is spraying — disconnect the return line and observe injector spray pattern.
2. Swap the ECU for a known-good unit to rule out ECU failure.
3. Contact the EFI manufacturer for an ECU re-flash with updated fuel maps.
